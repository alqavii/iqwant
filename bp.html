Technical interview question bank

Questions typical of oil trading technical rounds. Answer out loud first, then open the outline.

Where is Brent today, what's moved it this month, and where do you think it goes?

Have a number (check it the morning of the interview), two or three drivers (OPEC+ policy, inventories, demand data, geopolitics, positioning), a view, and what would make you wrong. Never answer without a current price.

What's the difference between Brent and WTI, and why does the spread move?

Different crudes and delivery points: Brent is a waterborne North Sea benchmark (with WTI Midland now in the Dated Brent basket); WTI is priced at Cushing, Oklahoma. The spread reflects US export economics (pipeline to the Gulf Coast plus transatlantic freight), US inventories, and Atlantic Basin balances.

What is Dated Brent and why do traders use CFDs?

Dated Brent is the assessed price of physical North Sea cargoes loading 10 days to a month ahead. Physical cargoes often price over a window around loading or discharge, so CFDs (contracts for difference against forward Brent) let traders hedge the timing gap between when a cargo is bought and sold.

Explain contango and backwardation. What does each say about the market?

Contango: forward prices above prompt, signals oversupply, encourages storage. Backwardation: prompt above forward, signals tightness, drains inventories. Link to the cash-and-carry calculation: spread vs storage plus financing.

What is a 3:2:1 crack spread and what drives it?

Refining margin proxy: (2 × gasoline + 1 × diesel − 3 × crude) ÷ 3. Driven by product demand (driving season, distillate for freight and heating), refinery outages and maintenance, product inventories, and export flows.

How would an OPEC+ cut of 1 mb/d affect prices, time spreads and freight?

Flat price up; spreads more backwardated as prompt supply tightens; medium-sour grades (which OPEC+ mostly produces) strengthen versus light sweet; long-haul tanker demand from the Middle East falls, but Atlantic Basin barrels may travel further to fill the gap, so freight can go either way. Show you think in second-order effects.

How does a physical trader make money if they don't bet on direction?

Arbitrage across location, time and quality: moving barrels to where they're worth most, storing in contango, blending to meet specs, optimising logistics and freight. Flat price is usually hedged; margins come from spreads and optionality.

A Suezmax freight rate is WS 90 on a flat rate of $18/t. What's the cost per barrel?

$18 × 0.9 = $16.20/t; ÷ ~7.3 bbl/t ≈ $2.22/bbl.

Why does BP have a trading business at all?

To optimise its own system (supplying refineries, placing equity production), manage price risk, and earn third-party trading margin using its scale, logistics and information. Its trading arm also supports gas, LNG and power positions. Tie this to why you'd rather join an integrated major than a pure trading house.

What does the energy transition mean for oil trading?

Balanced answer: oil demand peaks and plateaus on uncertain timelines, so volatility and regional dislocations may increase, which is good for trading. Growth areas: LNG, power, biofuels and SAF, carbon markets. Avoid preaching either way; show you understand both the commercial and policy sides.

Tell me about a trade you'd put on today.

Instrument, direction, size relative to risk, entry, thesis, catalyst, stop or invalidation, and how you'd hedge the parts you don't want exposure to. A spread trade with a clear fundamental reason often impresses more than a flat-price punt.

Quick-fire: how many barrels in a VLCC? What's 1 mb/d for a year? How many gallons in a barrel?

~2m bbl. 365m bbl. 42 gallons. Practise these until instant: oil mental maths.
